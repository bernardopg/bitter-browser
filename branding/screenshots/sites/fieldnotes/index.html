The quiet web: reading without being read

What changes when a page loads only what you asked for — no trackers, no autoplay, no sugar coating.

By Marina Duarte · November 2026

For most of the last decade a single news article quietly loaded more code than the first web browsers could hold. Most of it was not the article. It was the machinery around it: scripts that watch where you scroll, pixels that report back, and ads that move the text as you read.

The fastest page is the one that only brings what you came for.

A browser can refuse that machinery by default. When it does, pages get lighter and calmer, and reading becomes reading again.
